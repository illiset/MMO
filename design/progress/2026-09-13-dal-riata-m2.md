### Slope audit — roads ≤ 12° (PASS)
Measured along the graded centreline, and again on the finished surface inside the 6 m carriageway:

| road | length | max along-road grade | carriageway slope max / mean | z range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bohermara Road | 2683 m | **10.0°** | 27.1° / 6.4° | 7.7 → 94.3 m |
| Boherath Road | 534 m | **8.3°** | 10.6° / 4.8° | 42.2 → 73.8 m |
| Boherath (shore leg) | 1097 m | **7.0°** | 7.0° / 3.0° | 3.7 → 48.0 m |
| Dunadd spur | 389 m | **4.4°** | 4.7° / 1.7° | 41.1 → 51.5 m |

Verdict: **every road is ≤ 12° along its length.** The one number above 12 is Bohermara's
*cross*-slope where it is cut into Grey Head's shoulder (5.9 % of that road's carriageway
cells); it is a road cut, not a gradient the player climbs.

Island-wide: mean land slope 11.7°, 8.3 % of the land steeper than 35° (that is Benlea and the
Camasmore cliffs, by design).

### Owenfinn River and Athgorm Ford
The map does not draw a river on Dál Riata, so its course is authored from the approved names
doc: out of Shanderry Forest, south down Kingshill's western foot, across Boherath Road at
**Athgorm Ford**, then east to the sea at Portcorr. 1710 m long, 37 m of fall, water surface
forced monotonically downhill. The valley is cut **before** the roads so the road profile dips
into it, and the channel yields to the road where they cross — that is what makes the ford a
ford instead of a 5 m gorge through the carriageway. Ford water depth 1.0 m.

1. **The client GPU-crashes in Dál Riata on this laptop.** `LogD3D12RHI: Error: GPU crash
   detected` at frame 122, ~12 s after possession, while still compiling shaders (35 shaders,
   25 textures pending on screen), 4.56 GB of an 11.6 GB budget in use. The player *did* spawn
   correctly at Lissban with a working HUD (see the in-game capture) — the world then died under
   it. Next step: relaunch with the low profile **after** a shader pre-warm, and if it still
   dies, halve the HISM instance count and drop the 62 skeletal hero trees first (skeletal
   foliage is the most expensive thing in the level).
2. **Benlea's rock reads near-black.** The MWAM rock layer uses `TEX_MWAM_Rock_col`; pushing
   `MW_RockColorCorrection` from 1.0 → 1.55 → 3.4 barely moved it, so it is probably not the
   colour correction but the layer's blend or the preset's rock roughness. Next step: open the
   island preset in the material editor and look at what feeds Rock, or swap the rock texture
   for one of the vault's Quixel rock surfaces.
3. **Camera zoom clamp — the blocker is broken open, the edit is not made.** This has been
   "GUI-locked, impossible from script" since 2026-07-06. It is not any more: the official MCP
   toolset `editor_toolset.toolsets.blueprint.BlueprintTools` has `read_graph_dsl` /
   `write_graph_dsl`, and reading
   `/Game/MMOKit/MMO_Logic/Blueprints/Camera/BP_WowStyleCameraComponent.BP_WowStyleCameraComponent:EventGraph`
   returns the whole graph as an S-expression. **The literals are right there:**

   ```
   (event EventZoomInOut (X)
     (Variables|Zoom|SetZoomAlpha …)
     (Variables|Zoom|SetZoomFrom (Class|SpringArmComponent|GetTargetArmLength …))
     (Variables|Zoom|SetZoomTo (Math|Float|Clamp(Float)
        (+ (* X 100.000000) (Variables|Zoom|GetZoomTo)) 100.000000 1200.000000)))
   ```

   So today's values are **step 100, min 100, max 1200**, and BeginPlay already sets the default
   arm length to **900** (Daniel's hand edit, confirmed in the graph). The change Daniel asked
   for is exactly three numbers: `* X 100` → `* X 120`, `100.000000` → `250.000000`,
   `1200.000000` → `1600.000000`.

   **I did not make it.** `write_graph_dsl` replaces the *whole* graph, and the two reads of the
   same logic (EventGraph vs the generated ubergraph) already disagree on argument order in the
   `Math|Interpolation|Ease` node — so the round-trip is not provably lossless, and this is the
   camera Blueprint. Rewriting it unattended, minutes before Daniel woke, was not a risk worth
   taking for three numbers. The asset IS tracked in git, so the write is revertible: say the
   word and it is a five-minute job with a walk-test check after it.
4. **Rinnbeg → Dunadd pathfinds only partially.** Re-run `w08_nav.py` on a fully built navmesh
   before blaming the terrain.
5. **Megaplant is skeletal-only.** Either run the editor's "Convert to Static Mesh" over the
   ~50 species variants (then re-run `gen_scatter.py` with the Megaplant paths restored — the
   species table is still in git history), or adopt their ProceduralVegetation plugin.

## A. The client no longer crashes in Dál Riata (MUST — closed)

**Root cause, from the crash log rather than from guesswork.** `Client58m3.log` at
`2026.09.13-11.01.18` (04:01 local), frame 122:

```
LogD3D12RHI: Error: GPU crash detected:
	- Device 0 Removed: DXGI_ERROR_DEVICE_RESET
LogRHI: Error: Active GPU breadcrumbs:
	Graphics: Frame 120 > SceneRender-ViewFamilies > RenderGraphExecute >
	          "DistanceFields" > UpdateDistanceFieldObjectBuffers > UpdateDistanceFieldAtlas   [Active]
	AsyncCompute: ... > FXSystemPreRender   [Active]
```

and, 6 seconds earlier,
`LogNet: UNetDriver::TickDispatch: Very long time between ticks. DeltaTime: 13.15`.

So it was **not** the out-of-memory the 4.56 GB / 11.6 GB figure suggested — that budget was
never close to full. It was a **TDR**: the machine is an *AMD Radeon 860M iGPU* (`Integrated
GPU (iGPU): true`, 512 MB dedicated + 11.9 GB shared, UMA) and Windows' watchdog on it is
`TDR settings OK - Level: Recover, Delay: 2` — **two seconds**. One enormous mesh-distance-field
atlas build for a 4 km island with 25,308 instances blew straight through that and Windows
reset the device. The 13-second net-tick hitch is the same stall seen from the game thread.

Two things also worth naming: the project ships `r.RayTracing=True` **and**
`r.Lumen.HardwareRayTracing=True` (`Config/DefaultEngine.ini` lines 70–80), and the log shows
`Requested compilation of Path Tracing RTPSOs (6 permutations)` on an iGPU.

**What was changed, and where.**

## B. The Phase 0A movement pass — now proved, and two real bugs fixed

The first shift could only say "compiles and binds". Driving the keys exposed three things.

**First, a harness limitation, not a game bug.** `postshot.py` posts `WM_KEYDOWN`/`WM_KEYUP`
with `PostMessage`. That fires **action** mappings — C, X, Num Lock and Shift all proved out that
way — but it never drives **axis** mappings, so W/A/S/D/Q/E produced no movement at all and the
first run read as "everything is broken". New `Tools/dalriata/n2_realkeys.py` injects real
scancodes through `SendInput`; the same keys then work immediately. Anything axis-shaped has to
be tested with it from now on.

**Second, a real bug: you could not stand up.** `X` sit calls `PC->SetIgnoreMoveInput(true)`, and
the only way back out was a stand-up test that read
`PC->GetInputAxisValue("Move Forward / Backward")`. The kit binds movement on the **pawn**, not on
the player controller, so that call returned 0 for ever — and logged
`Request for value of axis 'Move Forward / Backward' returning 0 as it is not bound on this input
component` **twice per tick**, thousands of lines per session. Sitting was therefore a soft
softlock (only X, C or Num Lock got you out). Fixed by reading the keys instead of the controller's
axis table (`TRForwardAxis`/`TRStrafeAxis` on `APlayerController::IsInputKeyDown`, which asks
`UPlayerInput` and so sees every key whoever bound it). The warning count in the client log went
from thousands to **0**.

**Third, `NUMLOCK` needs no extended-key flag** — scancode `0x45` with `KEYEVENTF_EXTENDEDKEY`
is Pause/Break, not Num Lock. Harness fix.

**Per-key result** (`Tools/dalriata/walk2/moveproof2.log`, one key at a time, each step marking
the client log and printing only the lines that appeared in its own window, plus a capture):

| key | result | representative line |
|---|---|---|
| W forward | PASS | `turn 0.00 fwd 1.00 strafe 0.00 … speed 400` |

| A turn left | PASS | `turn -1.00 …` |

`UTRStartPlacementSubsystem` (`Source/MMOKitEval/TRStartPlacement.{h,cpp}`), a world subsystem
that runs on the authority only. On login it:

1. loads the zone rules for the current map by globbing `Content/Data/zones/*.json` and matching
   the `level` field (new file `dal-riata-starts.json` — the machine twin of this repo's
   `data/zones/dal-riata.json` `byArchetype`, plus a classtype→archetype table and a
   `realm/race/classtype` override hook that is deliberately empty today);
2. indexes the level: **5 PlayerStart tags found (dalriata, lissban, rinnbeg, tobarglas,
   dromcairn)** and the zone bounds read off the landscape actor by class *name*, so the server
   build needs no Landscape module dependency;
3. decides whether this is a first entry, and if so moves the pawn.

**Where it hooks.** `AGameModeBaseCpp::ContinuePlayerStart` — the kit plugin function every
world-map Blueprint calls once it has finished its own persistence fetch and spawned the pawn,
and the last moment before `FinishRestartPlayer` where the transform can still be corrected.
`RestartPlayerAtPlayerStart` is too early (no pawn, and its `StartSpot` is discarded by the kit).
The kit calls into our module **by reflection**, so neither module gains a dependency on the other
— the same trick the rest of this codebase uses in the other direction.

**The first-entry rule, and why it is not literally "Zone is empty".** The kit never writes
`Zone`: it is `""` for *every* character in `mmokit.db`, including Aldric. Firing on "Zone empty"
alone would relocate Daniel to a start on every single login. So the server tests **Zone empty
*or* mismatched, OR the saved transform unusable for this zone** — at the origin, or outside the
landscape's XY bounds. A normal login keeps its saved transform and logs that it did.

**Evidence** (`Tools/dalriata/walk2/firstentry2.log`, `Saved/Logs/WorldServerDR.log`). Celtictest's
`Zone` and transform were blanked in `mmokit.db` with `Tools/dalriata/n2_clear_zone.py`
(PersistenceServer stopped), then the stack was brought up:

```

          12 classtypes, 4 archetypes, 0 overrides
[TRStart] level indexed: 5 PlayerStart tags (dalriata, lissban, rinnbeg, tobarglas, dromcairn);
          zone bounds IsValid=true, Min=(-201600, -126000, -6088), Max=(201600, 126000, 24399)
[TRStart] login options (from InitNewPlayer): ?cookie=…?charname=Celtictest?charid=2
          ?trrealm=great-north?trclass=frontline?trrace=celtic…
[TRStart] login 'Celtictest' realm='great-north' race='celtic' classtype='frontline' zone=''

[TRStart] FIRST ENTRY: placed 'Celtictest' (great-north/celtic/frontline) at start 'lissban'
          [PlayerStart_4] (-91191, -31238, 3921) yaw -52.8 - was (0, 0, -90)
```

**The honest caveat.** The *decision* is server-side; the *identity* currently rides the travel
URL. The dedicated world server has no account session of its own, and the persistence server's
`GetCharacterMeta` (RPC 13) is account-scoped (`RPCs/GetCharacterMeta.cs` → `GetCharacterMeta(accountId)`),
so it cannot be asked "who is charid 2?". `TRStartFlowWidget` therefore appends
`?trrealm=…?trclass=…?trrace=…` from the entry the persistence server already handed the client,
and `AGameModeBaseCpp::InitNewPlayer` stashes the option string for the placement pass.
(`UNetConnection::RequestURL` was empty by the time `ContinuePlayerStart` runs — the first attempt
fell through to the default archetype and got the right answer by luck; that is why the log now
prints where the options came from.) **A client could lie about its class today.** The clean fix
is a charid-keyed RPC in `MMOKitPersistence` — a small C# addition, and the one thing this lane
still owes.

## D. Camera zoom clamp — done, and measured in game

Backed up first (`C:/dev/art-drop/backup/BP_WowStyleCameraComponent.uasset.n2-pre-zoom`), and the
asset is git-tracked besides.

**The whole-graph DSL round trip is genuinely broken — that is now proven, not suspected.**
`write_graph_dsl` on the text `read_graph_dsl` had just produced fails with:

```
AssertionError: The node could not be created / Input|SetInputMode_GameOnly does not exist
  in: (Input|SetInputMode_GameOnly _ownercontroller false)
  in: (event EventChatLostFocus)
```

The reader emits a node the writer cannot recreate, so rewriting the graph would have dropped
`EventChatLostFocus`. The first shift's instinct was right.

**What worked instead:** the same toolset has surgical tools — `find_nodes`, `get_node_infos`,
`get_pin_value`, `set_pin_value`, `compile_blueprint`. Three literal pins, nothing else:

| node | pin | was | now |
|---|---|---|---|
| `K2Node_PromotableOperator_5` | 1 (B, the `X * step`) | 100 | **120** |
| `K2Node_CallFunction_42` | 1 (Clamp Min) | 100 | **250** |
| `K2Node_CallFunction_42` | 2 (Clamp Max) | 1200 | **1600** |

A full `read_graph_dsl` before and after diffs to exactly one line:

```
-  (Variables|Zoom|SetZoomTo (Math|Float|Clamp(Float) (+ (* X 100.000000) …) 100.000000 1200.000000)))
+  (Variables|Zoom|SetZoomTo (Math|Float|Clamp(Float) (+ (* X 120.000000) …) 250.000000 1600.000000)))
```

BeginPlay's `SetTargetArmLength 900` was left alone — that is already Daniel's default.

**Proof in game, without a debug build.** The arm length is not readable at runtime from outside
the Blueprint, but the *clamp* is: step out one wheel click at a time and the picture stops moving
the moment the clamp bites. Frame-to-frame mean absolute difference over the sweep
(`Tools/dalriata/walk2/cam/`):

```
zoom out:  clicks 1-6  MAD 9.8 10.1 10.2 10.0 9.9 8.6   |  click 7 onward  0.19 0.14 0.13 0.13 …
zoom in :  clicks 1-12 MAD 9.0 … 11.9 6.9                |  click 13 onward 0.70 0.35 0.38 0.48
```

Six clicks out, twelve clicks in, then only ambient noise (clouds and grass). From the 900
default with **step 120 / max 1600** that is (1600−900)/120 = 5.8 → **6**, and 1600→250 is
(1600−250)/120 = 11.25 → **12**. The old values would have given **3** and 11. The measurement
picks the new clamp and rejects the old one.
Contact strip: `2026-09-13-dal-riata-n2-camera-zoom-range.jpg` (default / fully out / fully in).

**One thing the log does *not* prove:** `[TRFeel][CLIENT] camera report: arm 600`. That report is
taken at possession, before the camera component's BeginPlay has applied 900, so it reads the
SpringArm's authored 600 and is not a measurement of the runtime zoom. Worth either moving that
report later or dropping it.

**Also found:** the editor's crash-recovery dialog listed **`BP_WowStyleCameraComponent`** among
its auto-saves, i.e. somebody had unsaved edits to the camera Blueprint open at some point. I chose
**Skip Restore** — the committed on-disk asset is the truth — but it is worth knowing that an
unsaved camera edit existed.

## Method notes worth keeping

- **`mcp_call.sh` cannot carry a payload with newlines or quotes.** The Blueprint graph DSL came
  back as `{"code": -32700, "message": "Invalid JSON body!"}` every time, because the argument
  crosses a command line. `Tools/dalriata/n2_mcp.py` posts the JSON in-process; use it for
  anything bigger than a few scalars.
- **`UWorld::GetSubsystemArray<T>()` does not exist in UE 5.8** — it is `GetSubsystemArrayCopy<T>()`.
- **The editor's "Restore Packages" dialog blocks the MCP server**, so a force-killed editor looks
  like a hung one on the next boot. `Tools/dalriata/n2_realclick.py` clicks it away (SendInput —
  `PostMessage` reaches Slate hover only), or delete `Saved/Autosaves/PackageRestoreData.json`
  before launching. Better: quit the editor with `unreal.SystemLibrary.quit_editor()`.
- **A live scene never produces two identical frames**, so "did the picture stop changing?" has to
  be measured as a mean-absolute-difference against the ambient-motion floor, not as an image hash.
  That is what turned the camera zoom into a real measurement.
