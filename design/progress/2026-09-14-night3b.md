## 1. THE FOREST — measured first, then fixed

I did not start by changing anything. I stood the three fir assets on flat ground next to a
graduated pole of 1 m cubes at 0/2/4/…/14 m and shot them side-on (`measure_ruler.jpg`,
`measure_trunk_base.jpg`, script `b02_treemeasure.py`). Two numbers came straight off the image
and they decided the whole item.

### (b) The sprawling white roots: an off-by-one in the seating rule, not a bad mesh

`SM_SilverFir_L_01`'s bounds are **z −2.04 … +26.24 m** — the entire root plate hangs *below the
pivot*. Night 3 seated each tree by its bounding-box **minimum** and then sank 11% of tree height
to compensate; that arithmetic leaves the plate straddling the surface, which is the sprawl in
`forest_interior2_s.jpg`.

**Seating the PIVOT on the ground buries the plate completely.** `measure_trunk_base.jpg` is that
tree with its pivot at ground: the trunk enters the duff clean, no roots, no gap. No geometry cut,
no trunk-only asset, no Geometry Script — and the 60 minutes budgeted for building one were not
needed. New rule, in `b03_gen.py`: **pivot at ground −0.30 to −0.42 m**, no bounds offset at all.

### (c) Bare trunks: the thicket was one specific asset

Read off the same ruler: on the 28.3 m fir the lowest branch mass sits at **~6.5 m**. That is
already a hall. The thicket was the **sub-canopy** — 109,204 instances of `SM_SilverFir_L_02`, a
16.2 m scan that branches from about 2 m, which is what put needles at head height in
`forest_ingame.jpg`.

So the sub-canopy now uses the **same 28.3 m asset at a smaller scale**, and its branch line
scales with it. Nothing was invented and nothing was cut:

The honest remainder: **9.4% of stems still branch between 3.5 and 4.0 m.** Those are the smallest

### The lighting finding

The first fixed forest came back **almost black** (`BEFORE_forest_interior.jpg`). The structure was
already right; the frame was at 5–10% brightness. Cause: night 3's exposure clamp set
**`auto_exposure_min_brightness` 0.8**, which caps the adapted exposure at 1/0.8 and stops the eye
opening in a genuinely dark scene. The clamp that protects against a white-out sky is the MAX; the
MIN can come a long way down safely.

`min 0.8 → 0.10`, bias `1.1 → 1.30`, sky light `1.6 → 2.4`, sun `7.5 → 7.8`.

**Evidence:** `BEFORE_forest_interior.jpg` → `forest_deep.jpg` → `forest_ingame.jpg` (in the play
stack, same profile Daniel plays on). Against the reference photos: tall straight trunks bare well
above head height, high crowns, ferns and moss at the base, dark wet bark. Honestly short of the
photos on two counts — the trunks are bare for their lower **quarter**, not the lower two-thirds,
and there is no moss *on* the trunks (that needs a second material layer or a decal pass).

## 2. THE WATER — five wrong causes eliminated, then the real one

`lake_final_s.jpg` was grey speckled asphalt. The new material fixed that in the **editor** in one
pass (`lake_editor_ripples.jpg`), and then the **play client showed a flat pale sheet anyway**, and
would not respond to anything. Rather than keep tuning, I eliminated causes one at a time. This
list is the deliverable:

| suspected cause | test | result |
|---|---|---|
| the fog pooling over water | relaunched the client with `r.Fog 0` | distant hills went crisp, **the lake did not change** — not the fog |
| base colour too light | darkened it 2.5× | **no visible change** |
| a mirror surface with nothing to mirror | roughness 0.085 → 0.64, specular 0.72 → 0.28 | **no visible change** |
| the wave normal streamed out (250 MB pool, `sg.TextureQuality 0`) | our own `T_TR_WaterWave_N` copy, `NeverStream` | **no visible change** |
| too much fill light on a dark albedo | sky light 4.0 → 2.4, sun 8.4 → 7.8 | water got slightly greener, still flat |
| **`sg.EffectsQuality 0` forcing `r.MaterialQualityLevel:0`** | relaunched with `r.MaterialQualityLevel 1` | **no visible change** — not the quality level either |

**The cause is MIP COLLAPSE on the wave normal.** The water is one plane 692 × 440 m seen almost
edge-on from a third-person camera. At a 9 m wave tile the per-pixel UV derivative is enormous, the
GPU picks the lowest mip, and the normal comes back **flat** — so `n.xy = 0`, every term that
depends on the wave shape collapses, and the surface is one colour no matter what the material
says. In the editor the camera sat 4 m above the water at close range, the derivatives were small,
and the ripples showed. That editor/client split is the whole mystery.

**Fix: tile size up.** Lake 900 → **3600**, sea 1500 → **7000**. The waves become long swells
instead of fine ripples — which is also what water at 100–600 m actually looks like.
`BEFORE_lake_lowprofile.jpg` → `lake_ingame.jpg` / `lake_ingame_zoom.jpg` / `coast_ingame.jpg`.

### What the material actually is now

`M_TR_Water`, rebuilt from scratch, **66 wires, 0 failures**, OPAQUE (night 3's finding that
translucency does not light here still stands and was not re-litigated):

- **world-space UV** from `AbsoluteWorldPosition`, so tiling is independent of the plane's scale;
- **three panning normal layers** at 1× / 2.5× / 5.5× the tile size, different speeds and
  directions, so nothing repeats visibly at 100 m;
- **a distance fade on the normal** — far water becomes a sheet instead of aliased speckle (that
  speckle *is* the asphalt look);
- **Fresnel darkening toward the horizon** driving a shallow→deep colour lerp, which replaces the
  DepthFade gradient that opaque rendering cannot have;
- **`WaveContrast`** — the crest/trough colour separation. This is the one that matters on a
  profile with no reflections at all: with `sg.ReflectionQuality 0` and `r.ReflectionMethod 0` the
  specular has nothing to return but flat sky, so the waves have to live in the **albedo**;
- **shore foam from the world-space ground mask's ALPHA channel** — the shore band night 3
  generated and never consumed. One sampler in the *water* material costs nothing; the sampler
  budget problem is the *landscape* material's.

Proofs at the low profile: `lake_ingame.jpg`, `coast_ingame.jpg`. With reflections enabled in the
editor: `lake_reflections_on.jpg`, `coast_reflections_on.jpg`.

### Two water bugs found and fixed on the way

1. **The lake plane overhung its basin.** Measured off the night-3 heightmap (flood-fill of every
   cell below the 58 m surface from the lake centre): the carved basin is 692 × 440 m centred on
   (−1830, 1600); the plane was 790 × 489 centred on (−1824.9, 1608.2), hanging 44 m past the west
   rim over the outflow valley, which is *also* below 58 m. That was the hard straight water edge
   lying across the grass. Plane resized to the basin.
2. **The sea-level fog layer whites out open water.** Night 3 added a dense second fog layer to
   make fog pool in valleys; over open water there is nothing to pool against, so it accumulates
   over the whole sea. Density 0.045 → **0.010**, height offset −1500 → −30, fog start distance
   1200 → **9000 uu**. This is a real improvement but it is *not* what was making the lake flat —
   see the elimination table.

## 5. MOB TURN RATE — and the log says exactly why they spun

No C++ ever set a rotation mode on a mob, so they inherited the kit Character default. The
take-over log records what that was:

```
[TRAI][TURN] Red Stag rotation taken over: was orient=0 ctrlDesired=0 ctrlYaw=1 rate 360 -> rate-limited
[TRAI][TURN] Red Stag yaw rate 360 -> 180 deg/s (state 0)
```

**`ctrlYaw=1`** — `bUseControllerRotationYaw` was true, which snaps the pawn's yaw straight to the
controller rotation with no rate limit whatsoever. That is the spin.

Fixed in `ServerWatchHostiles`, once per mob, using the engine's own rate-limited path rather than
a hand-rolled `SetActorRotation` that would fight CharacterMovement every frame:
`bUseControllerRotationYaw = false`, `bOrientRotationToMovement = false`,
`bUseControllerDesiredRotation = true`, and `RotationRate.Yaw` set per state — **180°/s while
wandering, 360°/s in alert / combat / return.** The controller rotation is already driven by the
existing `SetFocalPoint(WanderTo)` / `SetFocus(Player)` calls, so both cases are covered.

**510 log lines on server boot: 255 mobs, each taken over and rate-limited, 0 failures.**

## 7. ONE MORE, FROM SHOOTING THE STANDARD POSES

With the six items done I re-shot night 2's standard island pose set under the new lighting, partly
to check nothing had regressed island-wide (`contact_n3b.jpg` — Lissban, Rinnbeg, Tobarglas,
Dromcairn, the Lissban three-quarter, Benlea, the river fall, the forest edge). Nothing had. But one
pose caught a bug nobody had shot before:

**Lissban's cabbage field rendered as a carpet of black blobs** (`BEFORE_lissban_cabbage_field.jpg`).
Night 3 placed 5,893 crop plants but only ever shot them from *inside* the enclosure, so the field
from outside the gate — the first thing a Frontline character sees — had never been in frame.

The crops' materials are plain `UMaterial`s from the Roman & Celtic pack, so the tint trick that
fixed the trunks and stumps was not available. It turned out not to be albedo at all: **it is
self-shadowing.** 1,664 cabbages in a 45 m plot each cast a shadow on their neighbours, and at that
density the field becomes one solid dark carpet. `cast_shadow` off on all four crop components
(wheat 1850 / millet 1856 / cabbage 1664 / flax 523) and the plot reads as rows of cabbages on
tilled soil: `lissban_cabbage_field.jpg`, `lissban_from_the_fields.jpg`. Crops at this scale have no
business casting shadows — the plot's shading comes from the painted tilled soil underneath.
