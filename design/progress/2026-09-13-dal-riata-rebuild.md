## B. Controls — IMPLEMENTED, BUILT **AND PROVED LIVE**

`Source/MMOKitEval/TRUIWorldSubsystem.cpp/.h`. Two builds, both succeeded (51 s, 56 s).

**Root cause of Daniel's bug:** `TickMovementPass` built the auto-run vector from
`PC->GetControlRotation()` — the camera — not the pawn's facing. New `TRTakeOverMovement()`
strips the kit's `Move Forward / Backward` and `Move Right / Left` axis bindings off the pawn,
turns off orient-to-movement and controller-yaw, and every vector now comes from
`Pawn->GetActorRotation()`. The `[TRMove]` line logs **yaw AND heading** so the fix is
greppable.

### Trades and iterations, all logged

1. **4 m/quad, not 2.** The 2 m grid is 7113 × 4065 = 29 M verts and the full-resolution
   passes were still running after 33 minutes. World **size is identical** (14 224 × 8 128 m).
   `TR_QUAD_M=2 python gen_terrain_v2.py` does 2 m/quad on the gaming PC (28 × 16 components,
   maths already in the script).
2. **World Partition is OFF.** `new_level(path, is_partitioned=True)` raises `TypeError` in
   5.8 — the kwarg is not exposed. The level loads and edits fine at this size and the client
   holds 22–30 fps, so the commandlet route was not needed today.
3. **Iteration 1** — the first relief render showed a flat southern plain and **ruler-straight
   road terraces**. Fixed: kilometre-scale lowland swell + ridged drumlin grain, ridges
   widened ×1.9 with long aprons, roads reduced to a shallow easing, pads tightened.
4. **Iteration 2** (coordinator caught it) — straight-segment lines still crossed the south.
   Root cause: `polyline_dist()` measures distance to a chain of **straight segments**, so its
   iso-lines are straight, and ridges, roads *and* rivers all stamped rulers. Fixed once for
   all three: every polyline is resampled to a **Catmull-Rom spline** and the distance is
   evaluated in **domain-warped coordinates** (±130 m of low-frequency noise). Third render:
   the south now reads as organic wear. Cost: the spline made the distance fields ~4× slower
   (10 min generation).

## Lighting, fog and the sea — three self-inflicted faults, all found and fixed

1. **A level made with `new_level()` has NO lighting at all** — the first captures had a black
   sky. Added an overcast rig: sun at −28° from the SW (intensity 3.0, weak, as overcast
   should be), sky light 2.6 as the real key, sky atmosphere, volumetric height fog, clouds.
2. **My own "widen the blends" change made the whole island orange dirt** —
   `MW_DirtSlopePower` 128 → 14 let dirt win everywhere. 72 is the working value; grass (3.0)
   and stones (1.4) keep their widened soft blends.
3. **Fog: three wrong turns before it was right.** (a) A single ExponentialHeightFog at
   density 0.035 with bright inscattering = a white-out at 40 m. (b) `LocalFogVolume` "banks"
   were worse, not better: a LocalFogVolume's height mode makes a slab with a **flat top**, and
   mine were hundreds of metres across, so their tops read as a horizon-wide sheet from 4 km
   away — removed. (c) An auto-exposure **clamp** (min/max brightness 0.9/2.2) turned every
   capture nearly black; those fields are not the EV range I assumed. Final: density 0.012,
   falloff 0.09, volumetric to 600 m, exposure bias −0.15, no clamp.
4. **I deleted the sea plane while chasing (3) and made it worse.** The pale sheet across
   low-angle shots at Lissban was not the sea — it was what you see where there is *no* water
   surface: the sky atmosphere showing through empty ocean. Lissban sits on Blunt Head's 900 m
   neck with sea on two sides, so most of that frame *is* ocean. The sea is restored (20 × 14
   km, `BlockAll`). A real animated water surface is still owed (focus list item 14).
5. **Ultra Dynamic Sky could not be used.** `Ultra_Dynamic_Sky_C` spawns, but a property scan
   found **zero** Python-settable weather/cloud/fog properties, so it cannot be driven
   headlessly and must not replace a rig we control. UDS integration is a GUI/next-shift item.

### Carrigrua — the castle on the crag (`v2_14_carrigrua.py`)

Daniel's vision doc asks for a real castle, not a fort: a large keep, an outer ward, curtain
walls all the way round, one way in. Built from the Medieval_Megapack modular castle kit that
was already in the project.

**The wall is not a circle stamped on the ground.** Every segment is tested against the terrain
first: the script samples the ground 12 m further out and, if it has already dropped more than
9 m, the rock is the defence and no wall is built there. That is the plan's own rule — *"you
can only wall what the rock does not already defend, and there is one way up."*

Placed: **49 curtain-wall segments with crenellations, 5 three-piece towers, one gatehouse with
portcullis and flanking walls on the single approach, a 9-piece cobbled approach road up from
the saddle, and a keep**. The keep is not dropped in the middle of the ring — the script
searches the ward for the **highest walkable ground** and builds there, which is where a keep
actually goes; it landed at (−1904, −1514) at 157.9 m. **Grounding audit on ground-seated
pieces: 67 actors, worst gap 0.000 m.**

Captures: `v2_carrigrua_3q_s.jpg` (it reads as a real castle on a rocky knoll, with the crag's
broken rock and scree around it), `v2_carrigrua_approach_s.jpg`, `v2_carrigrua_gorge_s.jpg`.

**Honest gaps:** the wall ring is still circular rather than following an irregular crag edge;

## The client crash at 17:13 — cause named

`Saved/Crashes/UECC-Windows-56C7B38148A677B031C9A2A9F3C60C41_0000`.
`EXCEPTION_ACCESS_VIOLATION reading address 0x30e`, render-thread breadcrumb
`RDGExecute_RenderThread`, stack all `UnrealEditor_RenderCore` / `Renderer` / `Core`.

**It was not a null mesh or material, and it was not the editor.** Two facts settle it: the
log captured in the crash folder is **`ClientV2c.log`** — the crash was in the *game client* —
and `MemoryStats.AvailablePhysical = 0`. I had the editor (5.1 GB), the dedicated world
server, the persistence server and a game client all resident at once, each with a 14 × 8 km
level and 159 k foliage instances loaded. The renderer dereferenced a null under host memory
exhaustion. **Nothing was lost:** `GN_DalRiata_v2.umap` is on disk timestamped 17:05, after
the spawner placement and the stats pass, and the editor process survived.

Standing rule from it, for this laptop: **never hold the editor and a full client+server stack
open at the same time on the 14 km level.** Stop the editor before a play test, or run
data-only editor steps with `-nullrhi`.

Related honest correction: **`LGT_DalRiata_Grass.uasset` was never created.** The step that
was meant to duplicate the grass type and restore its density failed on a property name
(`grass_types` vs `grass_varieties`) and the retry never ran, so the landscape grass still
comes from the SHARED pack asset. That is also why the forest floor could not be de-grassed
per-zone.
