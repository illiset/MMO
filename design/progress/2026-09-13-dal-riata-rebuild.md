## B. Controls — WoW spec IMPLEMENTED AND BUILT (not yet harness-proved)

`Source/MMOKitEval/TRUIWorldSubsystem.cpp/.h`. Build: **Succeeded, 51 s.**

**Root cause of Daniel's bug** (auto-run + LMB orbit → *"runs LEFT while facing FORWARD"*):
`TickMovementPass` built the auto-run vector from `PC->GetControlRotation()` — the camera —
instead of the pawn's facing. The kit's own `Move Forward / Backward` and `Move Right / Left`
axis bindings on `BP_PlayerCharacter` have the same defect for ordinary walking, and that
Blueprint is not greppable or safely editable headlessly.

**Fix:** a new `TRTakeOverMovement(APawn*)` runs once per possessed pawn and
- strips those two kit axis bindings off the pawn's `InputComponent`,
- sets `bUseControllerRotationYaw = false` and `bOrientRotationToMovement = false` (otherwise
  the body swings into a strafe, or glues itself to the camera and breaks LMB-orbit-only),

and `TickMovementPass` now builds **every** movement vector from
`Pawn->GetActorRotation()`:

| Input | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| W / S | forward / back along **facing** |
| Q / E | strafe relative to **facing** |
| A / D | turn the **character**; control yaw gets the same delta so the camera follows the turn and any LMB orbit offset is preserved rather than snapped away |
| LMB drag | camera orbit **only** — facing and movement unchanged |
| RMB drag | turns the **character** (body yaw snapped to control yaw, camera ends up behind) |
| LMB + RMB | move forward **and cancel** Num Lock auto-run |
| S | cancels auto-run |
| Num Lock | auto-run, in the **facing** direction |

The 2 Hz `[TRMove][CLIENT]` evidence line now prints **both `yaw` and `heading`** (the
direction the body is actually travelling). With the fix those two must track each other —
that equality is what a harness run should grep for.

**NOT DONE: the harness proof.** The build is in, the logic is in, but no server+client run
was made — the time went to terrain and vegetation. This is the first thing to run next
shift: `Tools/dalriata/n2_realkeys.py hold W D 3` and auto-run + an LMB orbit, then check
`yaw` vs `heading` in the client log.

## C. Terrain — REGENERATED, natural by construction

`Tools/dalriata/v2/gen_terrain_v2.py`. Pipeline: domain-warped fBm + **ridged multifractal**
→ plan landforms blended in (each noise-modulated and warped, never a stamped ellipse) →
**hydraulic erosion (900 000 droplets with sediment transport)** → **thermal erosion (40
iterations, 34° talus)** → slope-modulated detail → drainage carved (Owenfinn, 5 streams,
the 26 m waterfall where the river leaves the forest, the lake basin) → gentle road easing →
coast treatment (cliffs at headlands, wave-cut platforms, uneven sea floor).

**Measured:**

| | v1 (3 km) | v2 |
|---|---|---|
| Land area | 3.43 km² | **44.0 km²** |
| Span | 2.87 × 1.79 km | **11.0 × 6.0 km** |
| Max height | 244 m | **571.9 m** |
| Mean land slope | 10.6° | **14.9°** |
| Land over 35° | 4.7 % | **12.3 %** |
| Erosion | none | mean **5.14 m** moved on land, max **105 m** |

**Imported into the editor and verified:** `DR_Landscape_V2` in
`/Game/ThreeRealms/Maps/GN_DalRiata_v2`, 3557 × 2033 verts, 14 × 8 components of 254 quads,
scale (400, 400, 256), height −69.3 m to +571.9 m, `importResult: ok`, source resolution
matched exactly (no resample).

### Two deliberate trades, both logged

1. **4 m/quad, not the 2 m/quad the brief asked for.** The 2 m grid is 7113 × 4065 = 29 M
   vertices, and the full-resolution passes were **still running after 33 minutes** on this
   laptop. The world **size is identical either way** (14 224 × 8 128 m); only vertex density
   changes. Shipping 4 m/quad meant the island actually exists in the editor today.
   `TR_QUAD_M=2 python gen_terrain_v2.py` regenerates at 2 m/quad on the gaming PC — the
   component maths is already in the script (28 × 16 components).
2. **World Partition is NOT on.** `LevelEditorSubsystem.new_level(path, is_partitioned=True)`
   raises `TypeError` in 5.8 — the kwarg is not exposed. The level is a normal persistent
   level. It loads and edits fine at this size; WP conversion is a next-shift item (the
   `WorldPartitionConvertCommandlet` is the likely route).

### One iteration, because the first render failed its own test

The first relief render showed (a) the southern two-thirds as a flat green plain and (b) the
road corridors as **ruler-straight terraces** cut across it — 40 smoothing passes over a 54 m
band. Both are exactly the "generated-looking" failure Daniel rejects, so the generator was
patched and re-run before anything was placed on it: lowland relief got a kilometre-scale
swell plus a ridged drumlin grain (+74/+62/+38 m components), plan ridges were widened ×1.9
with a long apron, road corridors dropped to 8 passes over a 15 m band at 55 % strength
(roads are **painted** in v2, so the terrain only needs easing, not a bench), and settlement
pads were tightened so they stop reading as discs. Second render: terraces gone, mean slope
13.1° → 14.9°.

## Vegetation — ecological placement

`Tools/dalriata/v2/gen_veg_v2.py` (offline) → `v2_03_veg.py` (editor, HISM).

Rules implemented: clustered placement (cluster centres + children, so the eye reads stands
not a grid); min spacing by crown radius so trunks never intersect; a **density gradient**
from a dense forest core out through a thinning rim to lone hedgerow trees in the meadow;
nothing on slopes over 35° or above the 420 m tree line; cleared rings around settlements and
along roads; understory only inside the canopy; reeds and birch along water; stumps only
where people actually cut (near roads and settlements, on ground under 12°).

**Trees at real size is enforced by MEASUREMENT, not a guessed multiplier**: the editor step
reads each source mesh's bounding box and scales every instance so its finished world height
equals the metres the generator asked for. That is the direct fix for *"the biggest tree is
about 3× the player"*.

**Asset findings.** No Norway Spruce anywhere. **Megaplant_Library whole plants are

`fern_exp5_SM` fallback. `ApexNature` holds exactly **one** rock mesh, so boulder variety is
poor. Converting Megaplants to static meshes remains the open pipeline fork (D-ART-1).

## Captures — and an honest verdict against Daniel's reference photos

Four fixed-pose captures at Epic scalability with gizmos hidden
(`Tools/dalriata/v2/v2_*.png`, small copies `*_s.jpg`; the design-repo copies are in
`design/progress/captures/2026-09-13-rebuild/`).

**The first set failed its own test, and the failure was instructive.** A level created with
`new_level()` has **no lighting whatsoever** — the sky rendered black — and the ground was
uniform orange dirt from horizon to horizon because `MW_DirtSlopePower` 128 → 14 was far too
aggressive a "widen the blend" change and dirt then won everywhere. Kept as
`v2_forest_interior.png` / `v2_benlea_midslope.png` for the before/after.

Fix (`v2_05_light.py`): an overcast PNW lighting rig — directional sun at −32° pitch from the
south-west, sky light at 2.6 as the real key (overcast means the sky *is* the light), sky
atmosphere, volumetric exponential height fog, volumetric clouds — plus `MW_DirtSlopePower`
back to 72 while grass and stones keep their widened soft blends.

**After (`v2b_forest_interior.png`) — this one is close.** Real directional light through the
canopy, fog greying out the trunks at distance, long tree shadows across the floor, a proper
scanned conifer trunk with bark relief at eye level. Against Daniel's photos it gets the
light, the fog, the trunk quality and the wet green.

**Where it still falls short of the references, stated plainly:**
1. **The canopy is not closed.** It reads as open parkland, not the reference's wall of
   trunks. The generator's core density is 270 stems/ha but the placement budget capped the
   canopy at ~25 k trees over a 12.9 km² forest, which averages far below that. The fix is
   not more code — it is a bigger instance budget in a smaller, denser forest core.
2. **The floor is grass turf.** The references show dark needle duff, moss and a dense fern
   layer. Grass should be suppressed inside the forest polygon entirely.
3. **Understory is barely visible** — the ferns and shrubs are there in the data but are lost
   under the grass layer and cull too close.
4. **The aerial capture is fogged out**: fog density 0.035 is right at eye level and wrong at
   4.2 km. Aerials need the fog temporarily reduced.
5. Benlea (`v2b_benlea_midslope.png`) reads as genuinely broken, eroded ground with outcrops
   and scree — that part of the brief is met.

**Verdict: the forest interior would not yet pass as a photograph of a PNW rainforest, but it
is no longer embarrassing, and the remaining gap is density and floor material rather than
anything structural.**
