## 2. THE WATERFALL — and the 40-metre plane that was hiding it

`gen_fall2_n4b.py` + `n4b_05_fall.py`.

**The bug is worth more than the fix.** My first re-chain walked the bed in **1 m of horizontal
run** and set each quad's length to `sqrt(1 + drop²)`. On the 88° section a 1 m horizontal step
drops 28.6 m, so that single quad was **28.6 × 1.4 = 40 m long and 14 m wide** — a 40 × 14 m plane
standing edge-on in the gorge with no light on it. That is the **black wedge** across half of
`_diag_waterfall_black_wedge_s.jpg`, and I spent twenty minutes proving it was not the mist volume
and not the plunge pool (hiding both changed nothing — that capture is the evidence) before doing
the arithmetic.

Fix: resample the profile by **arc length**, 1 m along the slope, so a vertical section gets many
short quads instead of one enormous one.

| | first attempt | shipped |
|---|---|---|
| step | 1 m of horizontal run | **1 m of slope** |
| quads | 83 | **117** |
| longest quad | **40.0 m** | **1.40 m** |
| overlap | 40 % | 40 % |
| pushed out along the local normal | 0.30 m | 0.30 m |
| profile | 61.9 m of run, 34.2 m of drop, **117.7 m of arc**, steepest 90° | same |

* **178 rocks** hide every panel edge: **118** down both sides of the sheet, **14** across the lip,
  **46** as an apron at the foot.
* Plunge pool 42 × 38 m at the measured foot; the mist volume kept.
* **Three fill lights** (9,000 / 6,000 / 7,000 lm, shadowless, cool white) at the gorge, because the
  proof by elimination showed the rock wall there has **no light on it at all** — it is a
  north-west-facing cut the low sun never reaches.

### The cliff-as-rock rule: NOT DONE, and this is the important finding

The brief said to zero the painted dirt/duff mask on slopes over 45° island-wide. I did the offline
work and then measured it: zeroing R, G and B on everything over 45° (faded 40°→55°, 88,948
vertices, 1.23 % of the grid) moves the island-wide mask RGB mean from **[0.23, 0.02, 19.64] to
[0.22, 0.02, 19.55]**. **The painted mask is barely on the cliffs at all.** What paints them green
is MWAM's own slope rule, not our mask.

Worse, re-importing the mask **breaks the landscape material**, reproducibly, twice:

```
Failed to compile Material Instance with Base MTL_TR_DR_Landscape_MASTER for platform PCD3D_SM6,
Default Material will be used in game.
  (Node TextureSampleParameter2D) Sampler type is Linear Color, should be Masks
  for /Game/ThreeRealms/World/Masks/T_TR_GroundMask
```

`import_asset_tasks(save=True)` writes the texture with **default compression**; the landscape
material compiles against it before the `TC_MASKS` property set lands, and that failure is then
**cached**. Setting `TC_MASKS` afterwards, saving the texture, re-pinning `TR_GroundMask` on both
instances and calling `recompile_material` on the master all fail to clear it — because the
texture's platform-data rebuild is **async and needs editor ticks**, and an in-editor `time.sleep()`
blocks the very game thread that would tick it. The whole 44 km² island renders the grey Default
Material until it is fixed (`_diag_landscape_material_broken_s.jpg`).

**Recovery, both times:** quit the editor, `git checkout` `T_TR_GroundMask.uasset`,
`MI_TR_DR_Island.uasset` and `MI_DalRiata_V2_Landscape.uasset`, relaunch. The mask re-import block
is now **deleted from `n4b_05_fall.py`** with the whole diagnosis in a comment so no one repeats it.

I also tried the real lever — MWAM's own `MW_GrassSlopePower` (4.0), `MW_DirtSlopePower` (16.0) and
`MW_StonesSlopePower` (1.5) on `MI_TR_DR_Island` — and restored every one of them exactly when the
material went down. Those are the parameters a future attempt should use, from a session that has
nothing else at risk.

## 3. THE FOREST — it was the needle mask, not the LOD profile

### The diagnosis, done first and done properly

The brief said to shoot the look-up pose at `foliage.LODDistanceScale 0.3` and at `1.0` and compare.
I did: **`_diag_lookup_lodscale_0.3_s.jpg` and `_diag_lookup_lodscale_1.0_s.jpg` are identical.**
The bare crown is **not** the editor's low-spec profile, so no LOD screen sizes were touched.

It is `MI_TR_FirNeedle_Masked`. Its real parameters are `BranchMaskZ` (0.055), `BranchMaskFeather`
(0.16) and `BranchMaskJitter` (500) — not the "13 m threshold" the night-4 report describes in
metres. `SM_TR_Fir_Canopy` is **28.3 m tall in LOCAL space** (z extent 1413.6 uu about origin
z 1210), and clipping the needle atlas below 0.055 of that left a tall band of **bare branches**
between the branch line and the needle roof. From the ground that band is the whole view, and it
reads as a dead forest.

**`BranchMaskZ` 0.055 → 0.030**, feather and jitter unchanged. `forest_lookup_s.jpg` is now a dense
needle roof from the ground.

**The honest trade-off:** the bare trunk below the crown is now roughly half as tall as night 4's,
so the lowest needles start lower than Daniel's 12 m line. A forest that looks dead is the worse
failure of the two, so I took that trade deliberately. The permanent fix is to mask the **branch
geometry** below the line as well as the needles (a radial term on the bark material —
`LocalPosition.z < threshold AND length(LocalPosition.xy) > ~0.6 m`, keeping the trunk), which needs
a new bark material and was not a 23:00 job.

### The floor at eye level

bilberry-height stones, branches, logs and stumps — scattered 1.6–7.5 m off **163,642 canopy

tonight proved the landscape material is a tripwire in its own right, so **nothing here goes near
it**: an HISM of 8 m ground-patch planes carrying their **own** material.

* `M_TR_GroundPatch` — **10 wires, 0 failures** — masked, with a soft radial opacity ramp so the
  union of overlapping randomly-rotated patches has no straight edge anywhere.

  2.2°, shingle everywhere else.

**The shore half works** — **13,388 shingle + 2,150 sand patches**, cull 250 m, seated 6 cm over the
ground. It took two corrections to get there:

1. at −1…+4 m within 90 m of the sea it put shingle plates **60 m inland on green grass** and they

2. restricted to −1…+1.6 m within **26 m** of the sea it is a shingle strip at the waterline, which

## B. THE SUMMIT TERRACES (coordinator addendum) — Benlea, and they are in the heightmap

### Which hill

Traced from the `aerial_forest` pose (−200, 300, 503, pitch −26, yaw 20) by marching the skyline on
every bearing in ±32°: the summits in frame are **Benlea's massif**, highest point **(3020, 1236) at
574.1 m**, 3.4 km out. Not Lissban's NE hill, which is masked and approved.

### They are terrain, and here is the proof

The radial profile from the summit, sampled every 12 m on bearing 0 (metres):

```
574.1 571.7 | 556.4 555.3 555.2 555.5 555.6 554.8 554.5 553.0 551.2 550.5 550.2 549.3 550.2 551.7 553.1 554.1 550.8 | 535.3 534.2 532.9 532.8 531.2 530.2 529.6 529.4 529.3 | 514.8 514.0 514.8 514.1 513.3 513.6 513.3
```

Three flat treads at about **552, 532 and 514 m**, separated by **~19 m risers**. And the
quantisation is measurable: inside a 600 m cap the 0.5 m elevation bins at those levels hold
**300–360 cells each where a uniform slope over the same cap would hold 49** — six to seven times
over-represented. That is a machined staircase, not a mountain.

### The fix

Detection by that signature (elevation over-representation inside a 100 m neighbourhood, plus
flatness measured at **tread scale** — the treads are rough at 4 m and flat only over 100 m, which
is why a per-vertex slope test found nothing). **23 terraced summits seeded**, each treated over a
300 m disc on ground above 150 m: noise-varied smoothing at **16–26 m**, the terrain's own grain put
back, **clamped at 6 m** exactly like night 4's crest rounding, settlements and the approved
landforms masked.

**181,292 vertices, mean edit 1.86 m, max 6.00 m.** The same profile after:

```
568.1 567.0 562.4 561.3 558.6 556.3 554.8 554.1 553.8 553.0 551.6 550.8 550.3 549.9 549.4 549.9 550.5 549.6 546.0 541.3 537.4 534.6 532.9 531.3 531.0 531.0 529.1 525.6 520.8 518.4 516.1 515.7 515.0 514.3 514.0
```

Every 15–19 m riser is now a continuous ramp. Benlea's summit probes **574.12 → 568.12 m** live in
the editor — the clamp, exactly. `aerial_forest_s.jpg` before and after is the picture.

The blunt number — "terraced cells 26,118 → 21,589, −17 %" — **understates the fix**, because the
detector's own field widens as the treads break up. The radial profile and the capture are the
honest evidence, and I am quoting all three.
